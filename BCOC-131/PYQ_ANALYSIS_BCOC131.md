## 📖 CHAPTER-WISE IMPORTANT QUESTIONS WITH EXAM-READY ANSWERS

### **UNIT 1: NATURE AND SCOPE OF ACCOUNTING**

#### **Q1. Define Accounting. Explain its objectives and scope. (12+8 marks) [Dec 2021, Dec 2022, June 2024]**

**Answer:**
**Definition:** Accounting is the process of identifying, measuring, recording, classifying, summarising, analysing, interpreting and communicating financial transactions and events in monetary terms.

**Objectives (4 main):**
1. **Systematic Records** - Keep chronological record of all financial transactions
2. **Ascertain Profit/Loss** - Prepare Profit & Loss Account to know business result
3. **Financial Position** - Prepare Balance Sheet to show assets/liabilities
4. **Provide Information** - Supply accounting info to users (owners, creditors, govt, etc.)

**Scope:** 
- Identifying transactions (via vouchers)
- Measuring in monetary terms
- Recording in Journal/Subsidiary Books
- Classifying in Ledger
- Summarising in Trial Balance → Final Accounts
- Analysing & Interpreting results
- Communicating through reports

#### **Q2. Distinguish between Cash Basis and Accrual Basis of Accounting with examples. (8/10 marks) [Dec 2021, Dec 2022, June 2024]**

| Basis | Cash Basis | Accrual Basis |
|-------|------------|---------------|
| **Revenue Recognition** | When cash received | When earned (right to receive arises) |
| **Expense Recognition** | When cash paid | When incurred (obligation arises) |
| **Outstanding/Prepaid** | Ignored | Recorded |
| **Profit Determination** | Inaccurate | Accurate (Matching Concept) |
| **Legal Recognition** | Not accepted by Companies Act | Mandatory under Companies Act |
| **Example** | Rent paid Jan 2025 for Dec 2024 → Expense in 2025 | Rent for Dec 2024 → Expense in 2024 (outstanding) |

#### **Q3. Explain Double Entry System. State its advantages. (5+5 / 4+6 / 10 marks) [Dec 2021, Dec 2024, June 2025, Dec 2025, June 2026]**

**Answer:**
**Definition:** Every transaction has two aspects - Debit and Credit. For every debit there is an equal credit. Total Debits = Total Credits.

**Advantages:**
1. **Complete Record** - Both aspects recorded
2. **Arithmetical Accuracy** - Trial Balance can be prepared
3. **Profit/Loss Ascertainment** - Nominal accounts show result
4. **Financial Position** - Real/Personal accounts show assets/liabilities
5. **Fraud Prevention** - Dual recording reduces errors/fraud
6. **Comparative Study** - Year-wise comparison possible
7. **Decision Making** - Reliable info for management
8. **Legal Acceptance** - Recognized by law

#### **Q4. Explain Qualitative Characteristics of Accounting Information. (10 marks) [June 2022, Dec 2025]**

**Answer:** (Per IASB Framework)
1. **Relevance** - Influences decisions, predictive/confirmatory value
2. **Faithful Representation** - Complete, neutral, free from error
3. **Comparability** - Across entities and time periods
4. **Verifiability** - Different observers reach same conclusion
5. **Timeliness** - Available to decision-makers in time
6. **Understandability** - Clear to users with reasonable knowledge

### **UNIT 3-4: ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES & STANDARDS**

#### **Q5. Explain Accounting Concepts and Conventions with examples. (10 marks) [Dec 2022, Dec 2023, June 2023, Dec 2024, June 2025, Dec 2025]**

**Answer:**

**Concepts (Assumptions):**
1. **Business Entity** - Business separate from owner
2. **Going Concern** - Business continues indefinitely
3. **Money Measurement** - Only monetary transactions recorded
4. **Accounting Period** - Life divided into periods (usually 1 year)
5. **Cost Concept** - Assets recorded at acquisition cost
6. **Dual Aspect** - Every transaction has two aspects
7. **Revenue Recognition** - Revenue when earned, not when received
8. **Matching** - Expenses matched to revenues of same period
9. **Accrual** - Recognize when incurred/earned, not cash flow
10. **Consistency** - Same methods period to period

**Conventions (Practices):**
1. **Conservatism/Prudence** - Anticipate losses, not gains
2. **Materiality** - Significant items disclosed separately
3. **Full Disclosure** - All material info disclosed
4. **Consistency** - Same accounting policies consistently

#### **Q6. Explain Matching Concept with example. (3+7 marks) [Dec 2022]**

**Answer:** Expenses of a period must be matched against revenues of the same period to ascertain correct profit.

**Example:** Salary ₹50,000 for March 2024 paid in April 2024.
- Accrual: Charge ₹50,000 to March 2024 P&L (Outstanding Salary)
- Cash Basis: Charge to April 2024 (wrong period)

#### **Q7. State objectives and significance of Accounting Standards. (10 marks) [Dec 2023, June 2024, June 2026]**

**Answer:**
**Objectives:**
1. Standardize accounting policies
2. Enhance comparability of financial statements
3. Ensure transparency and reliability
4. Prevent manipulation of accounts
5. Guide auditors in verification

**Significance:**
- Uniformity in reporting
- Investor confidence
- Legal compliance
- International comparability (IFRS convergence)

#### **Q8. Distinguish between Indian AS and IFRS / Explain Ind AS. (10 marks) [June 2022, June 2023, June 2026]**

**Answer:**
| Aspect | Indian AS (Ind AS) | IFRS |
|--------|-------------------|------|
| **Issued by** | MCA (Govt of India) | IASB |
| **Applicability** | Listed cos, banks, NBFCs, net worth ≥₹250Cr | 140+ countries |
| **Basis** | Converged with IFRS | Original standards |
| **Carve-outs** | Some modifications for Indian context | None |
| **Examples** | Ind AS 115 (Revenue), Ind AS 116 (Leases) | IFRS 15, IFRS 16 |

### **UNIT 7: TRIAL BALANCE & RECTIFICATION OF ERRORS**

#### **Q9. Rectify the following errors and prepare Suspense Account. (15+5 / 20 marks) [APPEARS EVERY YEAR - Dec 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, June 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026]**

**Standard Format Answer:**

**Step 1: Analyze each error - Identify accounts affected**
**Step 2: Determine if error affects Trial Balance (one-sided) or not (two-sided)**
**Step 3: Pass Rectifying Journal Entry**
**Step 4: Prepare Suspense Account**

**Common Error Types & Rectification:**

| Error Type | Example | Rectifying Entry |
|------------|---------|------------------|
| **Error of Omission** | Sale to Ram ₹500 not recorded | Ram Dr. 500 / To Sales 500 |
| **Error of Commission** | Sold to Shyam ₹500 posted to Ram | Shyam Dr. 500 / To Ram 500 |
| **Error of Principle** | Machinery purchased debited to Purchases | Machinery Dr. / To Purchases |
| **Compensating Errors** | Sales overcast ₹100, Purchases overcast ₹100 | No single entry; correct both |
| **Wrong Amount Posted** | Sale ₹500 posted as ₹50 | Debtor Dr. 450 / To Sales 450 |
| **Wrong Side Posted** | Cash received credited instead of debited | Cash Dr. (double) / To Party |
| **Subsidiary Book Errors** | Sales Book undercast ₹100 | Sales Dr. 100 / To Suspense 100 |

**Suspense Account Format:**
```
Dr.                              SUSPENSE ACCOUNT                         Cr.
Date    Particulars           J.F.   Amount    Date    Particulars           J.F.   Amount
       To Difference in TB                              By Errors Rectified
       To Specific Errors                              By Balance c/d
```

#### **Q10. Types of Errors and their effect on Trial Balance Agreement. (20 marks) [June 2026]**

**Answer:**

| Error Type | Effect on TB | Rectification |
|------------|--------------|---------------|
| **Errors NOT affecting TB** (Two-sided) | TB agrees | Journal entry (no Suspense) |
| - Error of Omission | No effect | Record missed entry |
| - Error of Commission | No effect | Reverse wrong, record correct |
| - Error of Principle | No effect | Correct classification |
| - Compensating Errors | No effect | Correct both errors |
| **Errors AFFECTING TB** (One-sided) | TB disagrees | Suspense A/c used |
| - Partial Omission | Disagrees | Complete the entry |
| - Wrong Amount (one side) | Disagrees | Difference to Suspense |

### **UNIT 8: DEPRECIATION**

#### **Q11. Explain Depreciation. Distinguish between Fixed Instalment Method and Diminishing Balance Method. (20 marks) [EVERY YEAR]**

**Answer:**
**Definition:** Depreciation is the permanent and continuous diminution in the quality, quantity or value of a fixed asset due to wear and tear, efflux of time, obsolescence, or depletion.

**Causes:** Wear & Tear, Lapse of Time, Obsolescence, Depletion

**Objectives:** True Profit, True Financial Position, Funds for Replacement, Legal Compliance

**Methods Comparison:**

| Basis | Fixed Instalment (SLM) | Diminishing Balance (WDV) |
|-------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Formula** | (Cost - Scrap) / Useful Life | Rate × Book Value (beginning) |
| **Depreciation Amount** | Constant every year | Decreases every year |
| **Book Value at End** | Zero or Scrap Value | Never reaches zero |
| **Charge to P&L** | Equal | Higher in early years |
| **Repairs + Depreciation** | Increases over time | Roughly constant |
| **Suitability** | Assets with uniform usage | Assets with high early efficiency |
| **Calculation Base** | Original Cost | Written Down Value |
| **Rate Determination** | Simple | Complex (formula: 1 - n√(Scrap/Cost)) |

#### **Q12. Machinery Account and Machinery Disposal Account. (20 marks) [Dec 2022, June 2024, Dec 2025]**

**Standard Problem Pattern:**
- Machine purchased, additional purchase, sale/disposal partway
- Depreciation at X% on SLM/WDV
- Prepare Machinery A/c for 3-4 years + Disposal A/c

**Key Entries:**
```
1. Purchase:  Machinery Dr. / To Bank/Vendor
2. Depreciation:  Depreciation Dr. / To Machinery
3. P&L Transfer:  P&L Dr. / To Depreciation
4. Sale:  Bank Dr. (sale proceeds)
          Machinery Disposal Dr. (book value)
          To Machinery (original cost)
          To P&L (profit/loss on disposal)
5. Disposal A/c:  To Machinery (WDV)
                  To Bank (expenses on sale)
                  By Bank (sale proceeds)
                  By P&L (profit/loss)
```

#### **Q13. Distinguish Depreciation from Depletion, Amortization, Obsolescence. (2+6+12 / 7+13 marks) [June 2022, June 2026]**

| Aspect | Depreciation | Depletion | Amortization | Obsolescence |
|--------|--------------|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| **Asset Type** | Tangible Fixed Assets | Wasting Assets (Mines) | Intangible Assets | Any Asset |
| **Cause** | Wear & Tear, Time | Physical Extraction | Time/Legal Expiry | Technology Change |
| **Method** | SLM/WDV | Units of Production | Straight Line | Sudden Write-down |
| **Example** | Machinery, Building | Coal Mine, Oil Well | Patent, Copyright | Old Model Machine |

### **UNIT 11-12: HIRE PURCHASE**

#### **Q16. Hire Purchase - Accounting in Books of Hire Purchaser and Vendor. (20 marks) [EVERY YEAR]**

**Key Calculations:**
1. **Cash Price** = Given or calculated
2. **Hire Purchase Price** = Sum of all instalments + Down payment
3. **Total Interest** = HP Price - Cash Price
4. **Interest Allocation** = On opening balance each period

**Books of Hire Purchaser (Asset at Cash Price Method):**
```
1. Asset A/c Dr. (Cash Price) 
   To Vendor A/c (Cash Price)
2. Vendor A/c Dr. (Down Payment)
   To Bank A/c
3. Interest A/c Dr. (Interest for period)
   To Vendor A/c
4. Vendor A/c Dr. (Instalment)
   To Bank A/c
5. Depreciation A/c Dr.
   To Asset A/c
6. P&L A/c Dr.
   To Interest A/c
   To Depreciation A/c
```

**Books of Vendor:**
```
1. Hire Purchaser A/c Dr. (HP Price)
   To HP Sales A/c (Cash Price)
   To Interest Suspense A/c (Total Interest)
2. Bank A/c Dr. (Down Payment)
   To Hire Purchaser A/c
3. Interest Suspense A/c Dr. (Interest due)
   To Interest A/c
4. Hire Purchaser A/c Dr. (Instalment)
   To Bank A/c
5. Goods Repossessed (on default):
   Goods Repossessed A/c Dr. (Agreed value)
   To Hire Purchaser A/c
```

#### **Q17. Default and Repossession in Hire Purchase. (20 marks) [Dec 2022, June 2024]**

**Scenario:** Hirer defaults after paying some instalments. Vendor repossesses goods.

**Accounting:**
1. **Calculate Amount Due** = Opening balance + Interest - Instalment paid
2. **Repossession Value** = Agreed value (usually lower)
3. **Loss on Repossession** = Book Value - Agreed Value
4. **Entries in Purchaser Books:**
   - Vendor A/c Dr. (Amount due)
     To Asset A/c (Book Value)
     To P&L (Loss on Repossession) *[if any]*
   - Asset A/c Dr. (Agreed Value) *[if partial repossession]*
     To Vendor A/c

#### **Q22. Normal Loss vs Abnormal Loss - Accounting Treatment. (10 / 7+13 marks) [June 2023, June 2026, Dec 2022]**

| Aspect | Normal Loss | Abnormal Loss |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| **Cause** | Inherent nature (evaporation, shrinkage) | Accident, fire, theft, carelessness |
| **Predictability** | Expected, unavoidable | Unexpected, avoidable |
| **Accounting** | Absorbed in cost of good units | Charged to P&L separately |
| **Stock Valuation** | Cost = (Total Cost - Sale of Scrap) / Good Units | Valued at cost (like good units) |
| **Journal Entry** | No separate entry | Abnormal Loss Dr. / To Consignment A/c |
| **Insurance Claim** | Not applicable | Claim admitted → Bank/Insurance Dr. / To Abnormal Loss |

**When Both Occur Simultaneously:**
1. First deduct Normal Loss from total quantity
2. Value good units at adjusted cost
3. Abnormal Loss valued at this adjusted cost
4. Insurance claim reduces Abnormal Loss

#### **Q27. Tally ERP 9 - Masters: Ledger, Group, Stock Item/Category/Group. (3+7+10 / 10+10 marks) [5 YEARS]**

**Groups in Tally:**
- **Primary Groups** (15): Capital, Loans, Current Assets, Current Liabilities, Fixed Assets, Investments, Branch/Divisions, Sales, Purchase, Direct Income, Indirect Income, Direct Expenses, Indirect Expenses, Suspense, Misc.
- **Sub-groups:** Created under primary groups

**Ledger vs Group:**
- **Group:** Collection of ledgers of same nature (e.g., Sundry Debtors group contains all customer ledgers)
- **Ledger:** Individual account head (e.g., Ram's A/c, Sales A/c)

**Stock Masters:**
- **Stock Item:** Individual product (e.g., "Laptop HP 15s")
- **Stock Group:** Collection of similar items (e.g., "Laptops")
- **Stock Category:** Functional classification across groups (e.g., "HP Brand" - can include Laptops, Printers)

**Units of Measure:** Simple (pcs, kg) & Compound (kg of 1000g)
