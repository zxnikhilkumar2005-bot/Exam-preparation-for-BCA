## 5.1 INTRODUCTION

The accounting process has **two stages**:
1. **Journalising** - Recording transactions in Journal (Book of Original Entry)
2. **Posting** - Transferring entries from Journal to Ledger (Book of Secondary Entry)

This unit covers both stages in detail.

---

## 5.2 WHAT IS JOURNAL?

### **Definition:**
Journal is the **book of prime entry** (book of original entry) where all business transactions are recorded **first** in **chronological order** (date-wise).

### **Features:**
- First book of recording
- Chronological order (date-wise)
- Both aspects (Debit & Credit) recorded
- Narration explains the transaction
- Basis for posting to Ledger

### **Format of Journal:**

```
JOURNAL
Date        Particulars                    L.F.    Debit (₹)    Credit (₹)
2024
Jan 1       Cash Account              Dr.              50,000
            To Capital Account                       50,000
            (Being business started with cash)
```

### **Columns:**
1. **Date** - Year, Month, Date (ditto marks for same month/date)
2. **Particulars** - Debit account (Dr.), Credit account (To), Narration [(Being...)]
3. **L.F.** - Ledger Folio (page number in Ledger)
4. **Debit Amount** - Amount debited
5. **Credit Amount** - Amount credited

---

## 5.3 STEPS IN JOURNALISING

### **Step-by-Step Process:**

| Step | Action | Example: Sold goods to Ram on credit ₹5,000 |
|------|--------|---------------------------------------------|
| **1. Analyze** | Read transaction from business viewpoint | Sale of goods on credit |
| **2. Identify** | Two accounts affected | Ram A/c, Goods/Sales A/c |
| **3. Classify** | Personal/Real/Nominal | Ram (Personal), Sales (Real) |
| **4. Apply Rules** | Debit/Credit rules | Ram (Receiver - Debit), Sales (Goes out - Credit) |
| **5. Record** | Journal entry with narration | Ram Dr. 5,000 / To Sales 5,000 |

## 5.5 COMPOUND JOURNAL ENTRY

### **Definition:**
A journal entry affecting **more than two accounts** - combining two or more simple entries.

### **Examples:**

| Transaction | Compound Entry |
|-------------|----------------|
| Paid ₹5,000 to Ram in full settlement of ₹5,200 | Ram A/c Dr. 5,200 / To Cash A/c 5,000 / To Discount Received A/c 200 |
| Received ₹4,900 from Shyam in full settlement of ₹5,000 | Cash A/c Dr. 4,900 / Discount Allowed A/c Dr. 100 / To Shyam A/c 5,000 |
| Purchased goods ₹10,000, paid ₹2,000 cash, balance on credit | Purchases A/c Dr. 10,000 / To Cash A/c 2,000 / To Creditor A/c 8,000 |

## 5.6 OPENING ENTRY

### **Definition:**
Entry passed at the **beginning of a new accounting year** to bring forward **assets and liabilities** from previous year.

### **Format:**
```
Assets Accounts          Dr.    (individual amounts)
    To Liabilities Accounts           (individual amounts)
    To Capital Account                (balancing figure)
```

### **Example:**
Opening balances: Cash ₹10,000, Stock ₹20,000, Debtors ₹15,000, Machinery ₹50,000, Creditors ₹12,000, Loan ₹20,000
```
Cash A/c           Dr.    10,000
Stock A/c          Dr.    20,000
Debtors A/c        Dr.    15,000
Machinery A/c      Dr.    50,000
    To Creditors A/c                12,000
    To Loan A/c                     20,000
    To Capital A/c                  63,000  (Balancing: 95,000 - 32,000)
```

## 5.7 LEDGER

### **Definition:**
Ledger is the **principal book of accounts** where all transactions from Journal are **posted account-wise**. It contains all Personal, Real, and Nominal accounts.

### **Format of Ledger Account (T-Form):**

```
DR.                              MACHINERY ACCOUNT                              CR.
Date       Particulars           J.F.    Amount (₹)      Date       Particulars           J.F.    Amount (₹)
2024
Jan 1      To Cash A/c                      50,000       Dec 31     By Depreciation A/c       5,000
                                                     Dec 31     By Balance c/d            45,000
                                                     ----                    ----
                                                     Total                   50,000       Total                   50,000
```

### **Key Points:**
- **Left side = Debit (Dr.)** - Write "To [Other Account]"
- **Right side = Credit (Cr.)** - Write "By [Other Account]"
- **"To"** on debit side, **"By"** on credit side
- **Balancing:** Difference = Balance
  - Dr. > Cr. → **Debit Balance** (Assets, Expenses, Losses)
  - Cr. > Dr. → **Credit Balance** (Liabilities, Capital, Incomes, Gains)

## 5.8 POSTING FROM JOURNAL TO LEDGER

### **Rules:**

| Journal Entry | Ledger Posting |
|---------------|----------------|
| **Account Debited** | Post to **Debit side** of that account (Write "To [Credited Account]") |
| **Account Credited** | Post to **Credit side** of that account (Write "By [Debited Account]") |

### **Example:**
Journal: `Machinery A/c Dr. 50,000 / To Cash A/c 50,000`

**In Machinery A/c (Dr. side):**
```
Date: Jan 1, Particulars: To Cash A/c, J.F.: 5, Amount: 50,000
```

**In Cash A/c (Cr. side):**
```
Date: Jan 1, Particulars: By Machinery A/c, J.F.: 5, Amount: 50,000
```

### **L.F. Columns:**
- **Journal L.F.** → Ledger page number
- **Ledger L.F.** → Journal page number

---

## 5.9 POSTING COMPOUND JOURNAL ENTRY

Each account in compound entry posted separately.

**Example:** `Ram A/c Dr. 5,200 / To Cash A/c 5,000 / To Discount Received A/c 200`

| Account | Side | Particulars | Amount |
|---------|------|-------------|--------|
| Ram A/c | Dr. | To Cash A/c / To Discount Received A/c | 5,200 |
| Cash A/c | Cr. | By Ram A/c | 5,000 |
| Discount Received A/c | Cr. | By Ram A/c | 200 |

## 5.10 BALANCING LEDGER ACCOUNTS

### **Steps:**
1. **Total** both sides
2. **Find difference** (larger total - smaller total)
3. **Enter difference** on smaller side as **"Balance c/d"** (carried down)
4. **Total both sides** (now equal)
5. **Bring down balance** on opposite side as **"Balance b/d"** (brought down) for next period

### **Balance Types:**

| Account Type | Normal Balance | Balance c/d on |
|--------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Assets** | Debit | Credit side |
| **Expenses/Losses** | Debit | Credit side |
| **Liabilities** | Credit | Debit side |
| **Capital** | Credit | Debit side |
| **Incomes/Gains** | Credit | Debit side |

---

## 5.11 SIGNIFICANCE OF BALANCE

| Balance | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Debit Balance in Personal A/c** | Amount **receivable** from that person (Debtor) |
| **Credit Balance in Personal A/c** | Amount **payable** to that person (Creditor) |
| **Debit Balance in Real A/c** | Value of **asset** owned |
| **Credit Balance in Real A/c** | Unusual (asset disposed, or liability secured) |
| **Debit Balance in Nominal A/c** | **Expense/Loss** (transferred to P&L) |
| **Credit Balance in Nominal A/c** | **Income/Gain** (transferred to P&L) |

## 5.12 TRIAL BALANCE

### **Definition:**
Statement of **debit and credit balances** of all ledger accounts to verify **arithmetical accuracy**.

### **Objectives:**
1. Check arithmetical accuracy (Debits = Credits)
2. Help locate errors
3. Basis for Final Accounts
4. Summary of all balances

### **Methods:**
1. **Balance Method** - Only net balances (common)
2. **Total Method** - Totals of both sides
3. **Compound Method** - Both balances and totals

## 5.14 KEY POINTS FOR REVISION

- ✅ **Journal** = Book of original entry (chronological), **Ledger** = Book of secondary entry (account-wise)
- ✅ **Format:** Date, Particulars (Dr./To), L.F., Debit, Credit
- ✅ **Narration** in brackets, starts with "Being" or "For"
- ✅ **Compound Entry** = Multiple debits/credits in one entry
- ✅ **Opening Entry** = Assets Dr. / To Liabilities / To Capital (balancing)
- ✅ **Posting Rules:** Dr. in Journal → Dr. side in Ledger (To); Cr. in Journal → Cr. side in Ledger (By)
- ✅ **Balancing:** Balance c/d on smaller side, Balance b/d on opposite side next period
- ✅ **Debit Balance** = Asset, Expense, Debtor | **Credit Balance** = Liability, Capital, Income, Creditor
- ✅ **Special Journals:** Purchase Book, Sales Book, Purchase Returns, Sales Returns, Bills Receivable, Bills Payable, Cash Book, Journal Proper
