## 3.1 INTRODUCTION

Accounting Principles are the **general rules and guidelines** that govern the field of accounting. They provide a framework for recording, reporting, and interpreting financial information. These principles ensure **consistency, comparability, and reliability** of financial statements.

Accounting Principles are classified into:
1. **Accounting Concepts** (Assumptions)
2. **Accounting Conventions** (Practices/Traditions)

## 3.2 BASIC ACCOUNTING TERMS

Before understanding principles, key terms:

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Business Entity** | Business treated separate from owner |
| **Transaction** | Exchange of value between two parties |
| **Assets** | Resources owned by business (Future economic benefit) |
| **Liabilities** | Obligations owed to outsiders |
| **Capital** | Owner's claim = Assets - Liabilities |
| **Revenue** | Inflow from business operations (Sales, Fees) |
| **Expense** | Outflow for earning revenue (Rent, Wages) |
| **Income** | Revenue - Expenses |
| **Gain** | Incidental profit (e.g., Profit on sale of asset) |
| **Loss** | Incidental loss (e.g., Loss by fire) |
| **Drawings** | Owner's withdrawal for personal use |
| **Voucher** | Documentary evidence of transaction |
| **Entry** | Recording in books of account |

## 3.3 ACCOUNTING CONCEPTS (Assumptions) 🔴 HIGH PRIORITY

Concepts are **basic assumptions** on which accounting is based. They are **not derived** but **accepted**.

### **1. Business Entity Concept**
> **Business is separate from its owner.**

- Owner's personal transactions NOT recorded in business books
- Capital contributed = Liability of business to owner
- Drawings = Reduction in capital

**Example:** Owner pays personal income tax from business bank account → Recorded as Drawings, not business expense.

### **2. Going Concern Concept**
> **Business will continue indefinitely** (no intention to liquidate).

- Assets valued at **historical cost** (not market/breakup value)
- Prepaid expenses shown as assets (benefit in future periods)
- Fixed assets depreciated over useful life

**Example:** Machinery bought for ₹1,00,000 shown at cost less depreciation, not at scrap value.

### **3. Money Measurement Concept**
> **Only transactions expressible in money are recorded.**

- Non-monetary facts (employee loyalty, brand reputation) ignored
- Limitation: Inflation not considered (historical cost)

**Example:** Death of key manager not recorded; but insurance claim received is recorded.

### **4. Accounting Period Concept**
> **Life of business divided into regular intervals** (usually 12 months).

- Profit/Loss ascertained for each period
- Financial statements prepared periodically
- Basis for accrual adjustments

**Example:** Financial year April 1 to March 31; Calendar year Jan 1 to Dec 31.

### **5. Cost Concept (Historical Cost)**
> **Assets recorded at acquisition cost** (not market value).

- Cost = Purchase price + All costs to bring asset to usable condition
- No upward revaluation for market price increases
- Basis for depreciation calculation

**Example:** Land bought for ₹50 lakhs, now worth ₹2 crores → Still shown at ₹50 lakhs.

### **6. Dual Aspect Concept**
> **Every transaction has two aspects** - Debit and Credit.

- Foundation of Double Entry System
- Assets = Liabilities + Capital (always balanced)

**Example:** Cash sale → Cash increases (Asset), Sales increases (Revenue/Capital).

### **7. Revenue Recognition Concept (Realisation Concept)**
> **Revenue recognised when earned/realised**, not when cash received.

- **Sale of goods:** When ownership transfers (usually delivery)
- **Services:** When performed
- **Interest:** On time basis
- **Commission:** When service completed

**Example:** Goods sold on credit in March, payment received in April → Revenue in March.

### **8. Matching Concept** 🔴 HIGH PRIORITY (Specific PYQ)
> **Expenses of a period matched with revenues of the same period.**

- "Matching costs against revenues"
- Accrual basis follows this concept
- Basis for adjustments: Outstanding, Prepaid, Accrued, Advance

**Example:** Salary ₹50,000 for March paid in April
- March P&L: Salary Expense ₹50,000 + Outstanding Salary ₹50,000 (Liability)
- April: Cash paid, Outstanding Salary cleared

### **9. Accrual Concept**
> **Recognise revenues when earned, expenses when incurred** (regardless of cash flow).

- Combines Revenue Recognition + Matching
- Basis for modern accounting

### **10. Consistency Concept**
> **Same accounting methods used period after period.**

- Changes allowed only if: (a) Required by law/standard, (b) Better presentation
- Change must be disclosed with effect

**Example:** SLM depreciation used for 5 years → Cannot switch to WDV without disclosure.

## 3.4 ACCOUNTING CONVENTIONS (Practices) 🔴 HIGH PRIORITY

Conventions are **customs/traditions** developed over time for practical application.

### **1. Convention of Conservatism (Prudence)**
> **"Anticipate no profit, provide for all possible losses."**

- Choose method showing lower profit/asset value when alternatives exist
- Create provisions for doubtful debts, discounts
- Value stock at **Cost or Market Price, whichever is lower**
- Do not anticipate gains (e.g., unrealised profit on unsold stock)

**Example:** Stock cost ₹1,00,000, market ₹90,000 → Value at ₹90,000 (loss recognised). If market ₹1,10,000 → Value at ₹1,00,000 (gain not recognised).

### **2. Convention of Materiality**
> **Only significant items disclosed separately; trivial items merged.**

- "Material" = Could influence user decisions
- Depends on size, nature, context
- Small items (e.g., ₹50 calculator) expensed, not capitalised

**Example:** ₹10,000 error in ₹100 crore sales = immaterial; ₹10,000 error in ₹50,000 profit = material.

### **3. Convention of Full Disclosure**
> **All material information must be disclosed** in financial statements.

- Financial statements + Notes to Accounts + Schedules
- Contingent liabilities, accounting policies, related party transactions
- Changes in accounting policies with effect

**Example:** Change from SLM to WDV depreciation disclosed with ₹ impact.

### **4. Convention of Consistency**
> **Same accounting policies consistently applied** (overlaps with Consistency Concept).

- Enables inter-period comparison
- Change only for valid reasons with disclosure

## 3.5 COMPARISON: CONCEPTS vs CONVENTIONS

| Basis | Accounting Concepts | Accounting Conventions |
|-------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **Nature** | Assumptions/Postulates | Customs/Traditions |
| **Origin** | Theoretical foundation | Practical application |
| **Flexibility** | Rigid, universally accepted | Flexible, may vary |
| **Examples** | Business Entity, Going Concern, Money Measurement | Conservatism, Materiality, Full Disclosure, Consistency |
| **Number** | 10 main concepts | 4 main conventions |

## 3.6 SYSTEMS OF BOOK-KEEPING

### **1. Double Entry System** 🔴 HIGH PRIORITY
- **Every transaction** recorded in **two accounts** (Debit & Credit)
- **Complete** recording of both aspects
- **Trial Balance** possible → Arithmetical accuracy checked
- **Scientific, systematic, reliable**
- **Legal requirement** for companies
- **Used by:** All medium/large businesses, companies

### **2. Single Entry System**
- **Incomplete** records (only Cash Book, Personal Accounts)
- **No** Real/Nominal accounts maintained
- **No Trial Balance** → Accuracy cannot be verified
- **Profit** ascertained by **Statement of Affairs** method (Capital comparison)
- **Used by:** Small businesses, sole proprietors

| Feature | Double Entry | Single Entry |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| **Completeness** | Complete (both aspects) | Incomplete (one aspect) |
| **Accounts Kept** | Personal, Real, Nominal | Cash Book, Personal only |
| **Trial Balance** | Yes | No |
| **Arithmetical Accuracy** | Verified | Not verified |
| **Profit Ascertainment** | P&L Account | Statement of Affairs |
| **Fraud Detection** | Easy | Difficult |
| **Legal Acceptance** | Yes (Companies Act) | No (except small) |
| **Cost** | Higher | Lower |
| **Suitability** | All businesses | Small, simple businesses |

## 3.7 ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES GOVERNING INCOME MEASUREMENT 🔴 HIGH PRIORITY (2/10 papers - 5+5 marks)

### **Principles for Measuring Accounting Income:**

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **1. Revenue Recognition** | Revenue when earned/realised |
| **2. Matching** | Expenses matched to related revenues |
| **3. Accrual** | Recognise when earned/incurred, not cash flow |
| **4. Consistency** | Same methods for comparable income |
| **5. Conservatism** | Don't overstate income; provide for losses |
| **6. Materiality** | Separate disclosure of significant items |
| **7. Objectivity** | Based on verifiable evidence (vouchers) |
| **8. Cost** | Historical cost basis for assets/expenses |

### **Objectives of Income Measurement:**
1. **Assess Performance** - Efficiency, profitability
2. **Basis for Distribution** - Dividends, taxes, bonuses
3. **Decision Making** - Investment, lending, management
4. **Accountability** - Management stewardship
